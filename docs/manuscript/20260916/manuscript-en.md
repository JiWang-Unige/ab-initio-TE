# Annotation dependence, transfer limitations and downstream utility of sequence-based transposable-element models

Working manuscript, 16 September 2026. Prepared by the project agent from qualified project results and the two Pro reviews summarized in this directory. It is a scientific writing draft, not a submitted article or a verbatim Pro-generated attachment. The new three-species library intervention remains pending and is described as such. Author details, final journal formatting, complete software citations and release assets remain editorial work.

## Abstract

Sequence-based transposable-element (TE) models can agree with annotation masks without recovering biological insertions or improving every downstream use. We evaluated a shared animal material detector, annotation-sensitive evaluation, representation learning, class/status prediction and a separate gene-masking workflow. The shared NTv2-based model achieved species-macro base-pair F1 of 0.8888 across six fine-tuning species, while comparator-run F1 was 0.4459. Its zebrafish–pig–chicken F1 ordering changed under common-prevalence reweighting, showing that aggregate scores cannot directly rank annotation completeness. Outside task fine-tuning species, reference-coverage gaps and model false negatives coexisted. On a 100-Mb simulation with known generating-library coverage, the fixed detector obtained F1 of 0.4506, below all five qualified traditional workflows, and showed no CPU speed advantage. Family-label-free NTv2 adaptation and complete six-species ontology evaluation resolved implementation and coverage questions without establishing consistent representation or classification improvements. In a separate GENERanno-derived masking workflow, Tiberius locus F1 improved over unmasked workflows on human, cattle and platypus panels. However, local losses remained, and comparisons with repeat-derived masks established neither superiority nor equivalence. These findings distinguish material detection, structural recovery, comparator dependence and task utility. They support bounded annotation-relative benefits while defining limitations that a universal annotation claim would obscure.

## Introduction

TE annotation combines sequence recognition with classification and interpretation of fragmented, nested and diverged genomic copies. Recognizing TE-associated bases, recovering contiguous annotated intervals, and identifying a historical insertion are related but distinct targets. A classifier can perform well on the first target while fragmenting or merging the structures required by the latter two. Likewise, adding sequence to a repeat mask is an intervention whose value depends on the downstream application rather than on a TE score alone.

Evaluation introduces a second distinction. A reference annotation is an observation generated with particular knowledge resources and algorithms. Unannotated sequence need not be biologically negative, and expanded reference knowledge can both support previously penalized predictions and reveal previously unmeasured model omissions. Incomplete reference annotation has long been recognized as a benchmarking problem ([Hoen et al., 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13100-015-0044-6)). The relevant empirical question is therefore how specific reference conditions affect measured errors, rather than whether missing labels are possible in principle.

Genomic sequence models offer an additional evidence source, but task labels, training exposure, calibration and test composition still constrain their interpretation. We evaluate these constraints through a shared animal detector and separate, explicitly identified experiments in typing, representation learning and gene masking. We distinguish L1 TE-associated material relative to a computational comparator, L2 contiguous comparator-run topology, and L3 biological insertion identity. A model score or adjacent pair of TE-like fragments does not by itself establish L3 or justify filling their intervening gap.

Our study asks which conclusions survive controlled comparisons and complete experimental denominators. Its contribution is the joint measurement of annotation dependence, transfer limitations and downstream benefit or loss across a bounded collection of workflows. The experiments do not constitute a single universal model: the shared detector, human source-model analysis, multiclass head, representation learners and GENERanno-derived masks have different histories and endpoints.

## Results


### Cross-species F1 rankings depend on reference prevalence and operating-point trade-offs

The shared NTv2-500M D detector achieved species-macro material F1 of 0.888761 on 500 internal DEV tiles from each of its six training species. Comparator-run F1 at IoU 0.8 was 0.445884 and IoU-qualified joint-boundary F1 at 5 bp was 0.204361. Thus, material agreement did not establish insertion recovery. All six species contributed to fine-tuning; the ranking is not an external generalization curve as a function of distance from humans.

Zebrafish, pig and chicken material F1 values were 0.927836, 0.893138 and 0.831720, respectively. Their reference-positive fractions differed substantially: 54.83%, 27.85% and 3.86%. Zebrafish had both higher reference recall and higher reference-negative FPR (9.16%, versus 3.49% and 0.61%). Holding the original recall and FPR fixed while reweighting to the three-species pooled reference prevalence of 28.8396% yielded F1 values of 0.863065, 0.895095 and 0.893513. The ordering therefore depended on the reference class mixture. This calculation neither corrected annotation errors nor identified their causal contribution; the historical scores remained unchanged.

We also bounded an extreme missing-label explanation while holding predictions and existing positive labels fixed. Even if every chicken FP base were confirmed positive, with no additional model misses exposed, its F1 ceiling would be 0.914999, below the observed zebrafish value. Pig could reach that value only if at least 72,733 bp, approximately 70.45% of its original FP bases, were confirmed positive without exposing any additional FN. These are optimistic arithmetic bounds, not observed rescue rates. They exclude this single mechanism as a complete explanation of the chicken–zebrafish score difference without ruling out partial annotation effects.

An installed Dfam 3.9 audit identified 1,717 curated lineage-specific entries for zebrafish, versus none for pig or chicken, alongside 3,047 and 177 uncurated lineage-specific entries for the latter species. Ancestor entries remained available, and historical probe logs explicitly recorded curated-only selection. This establishes unequal reference-resource conditions, not an ordering of biological annotation completeness. A finite three-species same-input library diagnostic has been initiated; its results are not included before qualification. The original D summary evaluator did not retain position caches, so the diagnostic requires a fixed-checkpoint replay that reproduces the historical confusion counts.

Sources: [original D metrics](../../experiments/CROSS-SPECIES-L1-UPSTREAM-20260904/seed42/D/dev_metrics.json), [retrospective diagnostic and figure](../../../reports/SPECIES-ANNOTATION-DIAGNOSTIC-20260916/RESULTS.md), [bounded library protocol](../../experiments/SPECIES-LIBRARY-CONTROL-20260916.md).

### Reference-coverage limitations and model misses coexist outside fine-tuning species

Frozen D recovered 95.93% of 1,483,825 reference-positive TE bases in four 1-MiB platypus regions. On four sea-urchin regions, the original curated comparator contained no qualified TE positives; an alternative uncurated-library annotation supplied 844,932 positive bases, of which D recovered 42.89%. Reference coverage therefore limited the original evaluation while substantial model misses remained under the expanded comparator. The original and alternative annotations differed in sequence context as well as library knowledge, precluding attribution to the library alone. The complete CB4 result below has an exceptionally sparse positive denominator and provides a third restricted external diagnostic. None of these species participated in the six-species task fine-tuning, but exact backbone pretraining exposure remains unresolved. Independent biological precision and F1 cannot be obtained by treating all unlabelled sequence as negative.

The subsequent sea-urchin adapter/MoE pilot used target-domain labels and calibration. It therefore represents supervised adaptation, not zero-shot transfer; the tested head MoE did not establish a consistent benefit over simpler head combinations and incurred structural or original-species retention costs. These results do not justify a universal animal model or immediate plant/fungal training expansion.

Sources: [fixed-D external diagnostic](../../../scripts/experiments/D-EXTERNAL-RC0-20260914/reports/RESULTS-20260914.md), [sea-urchin sensitivity](../../../scripts/experiments/D-EXTERNAL-RC0-20260914/reports/LIBRARY-SENSITIVITY-20260914.md), [adapter/MoE pilot](../../experiments/D-ADAPTER-MOE-PILOT-20260914.md).

### Library configurations alter annotation support without establishing false-positive rescue

We compared two repeat-library configurations on the same 2,500 hg19 windows from chromosomes 2–4, comprising 20,480,000 centre bases. Both annotations used identical input sequences, 4,096-bp flanks, RepeatMasker 4.2.2 and settings. The libraries were a global Dfam/RepBase release-20181026 collection and a curated human-lineage Dfam 3.9 export. The contrast held sequence and annotation engine fixed but jointly changed release date, taxonomic scope, curation and RepBase content; it was not a release-date-only intervention. The broader 2018 configuration provided more absolute support for apparent false positives than the newer, narrower export.

A source-only analysis without control reuse matched 5,540 of 21,235 eligible apparent false-positive intervals, retaining 15,695 unmatched cases. At the prespecified 50% and 80% interval-coverage levels, matched false-positive-minus-background support differences were 2.65 and 2.85 percentage points with the 2018 library, and 4.98 and 4.49 percentage points with curated Dfam 3.9. At the any-overlap level, differences were −0.67 and +1.81 percentage points. These descriptive contrasts concern the matchable population; unique controls do not remove spatial or homology dependence. An orthogonal RepeatPeps search provided strong support for 4 of 21,235 apparent false-positive candidates and none of 5,540 background controls; neither group had a strong hit within matched pairs. Of 26,775 queries, 21,060 were shorter than 30 bp, limiting interpretation of absent protein support. We did not revise frozen source labels or infer a corrected model F1 or independent biological-confirmation rate.

Source: [library-control result](../../../reports/ANNOTATION-LIBRARY-CONTROL-20260915/RESULTS.md) and its protein-evidence report. For plotting, `difference_pp` uses matched pairs, whereas `fp_supported` and `fp_fraction_of_all_cases` concern all eligible FP cases; these denominators must not be interchanged.

### Updating the final two NTv2 blocks did not produce a consistent clustering improvement

Six arms evaluated family-label-free representation learning on natural annotated copies after exclusion of the previously examined query set and recorded homology or local-neighbourhood overlaps. The retained 1,331 records were divided into TRAIN, CAL and EVAL sets of 806, 266 and 259 records using source-copy, recorded homology-component and 50-kb locus-block grouping. The upstream panel had been selected using family annotations. This therefore tests TE-enriched, family-label-free adaptation, rather than fully unsupervised discovery or species-held-out generalization. Training used two views of the same sequence, 2,000 updates and seed 42. A common cluster count of 20 was selected from a predefined grid using raw frozen-NTv2 TRAIN features and applied to all arms; this does not constitute a representation-neutral optimization of clustering.

The encoder-adaptation arm updated layers 27 and 28, with 33,570,816 trainable backbone parameters, together with a projection head. The saved-state audit confirmed changes in all 28 weight tensors from these layers. Transductive EVAL ARI increased from 0.236074 for raw frozen NTv2 to 0.256677, while NMI decreased from 0.615995 to 0.612937. Inductive ARI decreased from 0.221511 to 0.216809; the k-mer SSL control obtained 0.236724 on that endpoint. Adaptation improved over the tested frozen-backbone SSL projection but did not consistently exceed the raw encoder and trained sequence-feature controls. The approximately 1.08-million-parameter capacity comparison matched k-mer and enlarged frozen-NTv2 heads, not the total encoder-update arm. These findings do not establish a generally superior family representation.

Source: [complete six-arm result](../../../reports/NTV2-LABELFREE-CLUSTER-20260915/run-12732191/RESULTS.md).

### Complete six-species evaluation resolves coverage and annotation-state ambiguity

The revised SF5 experiment used eight class/status labels and the complete six-species allocation: 5,400 training, 1,440 validation and 2,160 test windows, with 900, 240 and 360 windows per species. Checkpoint selection used the complete validation set. Across 8,847,360 test positions, comparator-material F1 was 0.884305 and pooled main-four-class macro F1 was 0.833687. The material endpoint grouped all seven non-background labels, including ambiguous and unclassified states. Background denoted sequence uncovered by the comparator, not independently verified non-TE DNA. The main-four score averaged globally computed SINE, LINE, LTR and DNA F1 values and was not a species-macro average.

The retained six-label checkpoint, evaluated on the same complete test set after collapsing the new statuses, obtained material F1 of 0.884531 and main-four macro F1 of 0.834947. It was not retrained or reselected with the revised validation protocol, so this comparison does not isolate a causal effect of ontology refinement. Complete coverage and explicit status reporting are established; overall performance improvement, specific-superfamily annotation and independent biological truth are not. Absent status classes were distinguished from measured zero recovery. Exact-label status micro F1 was not substituted for the joint confusion counts required for precise collapsed-status binary F1.

Source: [SF5 result and per-species supports](../../../reports/SF5-ONTOLOGY-CLOSURE-20260915/run-12731987/RESULTS.md).

### Long-input evaluation exposes limited transfer and no observed CPU advantage for frozen D

On the 100,000,000-bp TE_Bench/GARLIC-derived simulation, the frozen shared D model obtained base-pair precision, recall and F1 of 0.887689, 0.301975 and 0.450648. The generation-derived strict TE-material denominator was 54,481,086 bp. Qualified fixed-RepeatMasker, RepeatModeler2-to-RepeatMasker, HiTE, EDTA and EarlGrey workflows obtained F1 values of 0.976293, 0.946999, 0.637386, 0.844020 and 0.974942. All 366 generating-library records could be matched by name or accession to the fixed RepeatMasker library. These records were not 366 independent biological TE families, and accession coverage did not establish nucleotide-identical references. This was a known-library challenge with favourable information availability for reference-assisted annotation. Those conditions restrict generalization of the ranking but do not remove the observed low recovery by D; model, calibration and threshold were unchanged.

On the 108,384,165-bp C. briggsae assembly, 105,416,539 bp were callable, but the fixed reference contained only 1,571 strict TE-positive bases from 23 SINE records. D recovered 972 bases and missed 599. Its remaining predictions were unlabelled, not established false positives or newly confirmed TEs. Fixed RepeatMasker's complete recovery reflected agreement with a closely related reference-generation procedure, not independently validated perfect sensitivity. Real-genome precision and F1 remained null. D's complete CPU workflow took 23,861.16 s on the simulation and 26,112.05 s on CB4, slower than the other qualified completed native CPU workflows in this comparison. GPU times are reported separately by device. This batch supports neither CPU speed advantage nor broad accuracy superiority.

EDTA failed its default complete-LTR-candidate check on CB4 and remains a failed method cell, without a zero accuracy score. Two historical EarlGrey continuations omitted the required starting lineage library from final RepeatMasker annotation; their scores are protocol-ineligible. The completed bounded correction restored the original library condition and rebuilt affected final stages while retaining previous elapsed time. Exact concatenation of strained and starting libraries was verified for both recovered cells. The final matrix contains 13 qualified completions and one native failure. On sim100, EarlGrey precision and recall were 0.979105 and 0.970814; the D-minus-EarlGrey F1 difference was −0.524294, with a within-input paired-block 95% interval [−0.531093, −0.517608]. A new pinned Omni workflow completed all four aggregation jobs from score bundle 12739923. This replay reproduced block-count metrics; it did not rerun native callers or independently establish biological truth. The old EarlGrey scores and their earlier replay remain preserved as protocol-ineligible historical attempts.

Source: [long-input result and qualification records](../../../reports/TE-LONG-BENCH-20260915/RESULTS.md).

### Human P3 masking improves pooled gene-prediction agreement but fails the loss safeguard

In the completed fixed human experiment, the P3-R1 mask increased pooled Tiberius locus F1 from 0.636307 with unmasked input to 0.699363 across 726 reference loci and 20 genomic cores. The difference was 0.063056, with the prespecified paired-core 95% bootstrap interval [0.036503, 0.106832]. P3 masking gained 55 correctly matched loci and lost 16, yielding a net gain of 39. However, loss of 16 of 510 previously correct loci, or 3.1373%, exceeded the prespecified 1% limit. The joint acceptance criterion remained unmet despite a positive aggregate effect. The P3-minus-UCSC-repeat-mask F1 difference was 0.003932, with interval [−0.002606, 0.013370], establishing neither superiority nor equivalence. This experiment used GENERanno-derived P3 and cannot be attributed to the NTv2-based D model in the long-input benchmark.

The completed external study evaluated 40 cores and all 200 prespecified cells across cattle and platypus, with 470 and 639 eligible reference loci, respectively. P3-mask locus F1 was 0.616466 in cattle and 0.589170 in platypus. Relative to the official unmasked workflow (U_nosm), differences were +0.067861 (chromosome-paired 95% interval [0.028046, 0.116576]) and +0.056828 ([0.028078, 0.082591]). The same-checkpoint comparisons against U_soft were also positive: +0.178815 ([0.147761, 0.207924]) and +0.031745 ([0.005494, 0.061528]). The U_nosm comparison changes the Tiberius checkpoint as well as masking, whereas U_soft isolates the mask input within a common checkpoint.

The prespecified P-minus-R_TE differences were −0.000917 in cattle ([−0.020464, 0.016219]) and −0.002563 in platypus ([−0.015224, 0.009415]). Thus, the experiment established neither superiority nor equivalence to the native RepeatMasker TE mask. Compared with U_nosm, P gained 53 and lost 31 correctly predicted cattle loci, and gained 90 and lost 40 platypus loci. Loss fractions were 10.88% and 11.17%, respectively. Against U_soft, these fractions were 2.57% and 6.30%; all control comparisons exceeded 1%. Such losses denote changes in exact reference-CDS-chain agreement, not demonstrated biological gene deletion. The original human safeguard decision remains unchanged.

These external results extend annotation-relative masking utility to two mammals under fixed protocols, not to all animals or to shared D. Each species used ten chromosome clusters and 10,000 paired bootstrap replicates with seed 42. Cattle had 220 loci with at least one NM transcript; platypus had none, so its corresponding subset recall remained undefined. Successful complete-input, native encoding and GTF/GFF3 checks establish execution validity, not an independent biological reference.

Sources: [human result](../../../reports/P3-TIBERIUS-BASE-MASK-20260911-R1/full-r1-score-12710872/RESULTS.md), [external protocol](../../experiments/P3-TIBERIUS-EXTERNAL-20260915.md), [complete external result](../../../reports/P3-TIBERIUS-EXTERNAL-20260915/full-r1-score-12740044/RESULTS.md).

### Additional gap information does not establish a safe gap-filling action

The earlier matched HN-O gap experiment combined nucleotide-transformer logits with seam features. Relative to its H0-O control, it reduced length-weighted negative-fraction MSE by 8.5905% and increased action average precision from 0.303599 to 0.387216 on 60,569 fully known development gaps. This information benefit did not yield an admissible masking policy: none of 60,497 tied-score thresholds passed even the optimistic necessary utility and negative-masking conditions. The result concerns this fixed score ordering and whole-gap policy family, not an impossibility theorem for gap resolution.

A separate truth-assisted human diagnostic evaluated nine cores and 27 gene-prediction cells. Adding comparator-positive whole gaps did not change the number of correct CDS chains; adding only positive gap bases changed chain F1 from 0.323353 to 0.324324 through one fewer false positive, without a newly recovered correct chain. The base-mask utility experiments above ask a different question and do not demonstrate that the gap problem has been solved. We stopped new gap-method searches for this manuscript while retaining these informative negative results.

Sources: [gap information](../../../reports/GAP-A-B1-SCREEN-20260908-R1/result.json), [fixed-policy feasibility](../../../reports/GAP-HN-RANK-FEASIBILITY-20260908-R1/result.json), [truth-assisted downstream diagnostic](../../../reports/GAP-BRIDGE-C-UTILITY-20260906-R1/result.json).

## Discussion

These experiments evaluate distinct targets rather than demonstrate completion of one universal TE model. Annotation support, material detection, structural reconstruction and downstream utility answer different questions and partly involve different models. Holding input and annotation engine fixed established sensitivity to two library configurations, but taxonomic scope, curation and database content remained confounded with release date. Matched support differences and sparse protein hits do not justify reclassifying apparent false positives as true positives. Conversely, incomplete natural-genome references cannot explain low recovery against directly constructed simulated material truth.

The negative long-input benchmark and positive human masking effect are compatible. The former evaluates frozen NTv2-based D under one simulated distribution and deployment calibration; the latter evaluates a GENERanno-derived P3 mask as input to a fixed gene predictor. Downstream gain does not establish greater global mask accuracy, recovered missing fragments or biological insertion identity. Failure of a joint acceptance criterion does not erase positive pooled utility; it records that gain coexists with loss exceeding the application safeguard. The original losses and criteria remain visible irrespective of later experiments.

Actual family-label-free encoder adaptation was implemented, but improvement depended on endpoint. Complete six-species classification repaired a coverage problem and separated annotation statuses, without establishing a general superfamily tool or a causal performance benefit from relabelling. These bounded findings need not imply that all genomic representations or future structural approaches must fail. The manuscript concludes within tested scope: no general accuracy or CPU-speed advantage, no independently confirmed false-positive rescue, and annotation-relative masking benefits across the tested human and external mammalian panels, accompanied by losses and without established superiority to repeat masks. These results do not justify selecting new thresholds, replacing test data or redefining negative outcomes.

## Methods

### Model identities, sampling and reference labels

The shared D detector used the NTv2-500M backbone with H0 initialization. Its matched upstream protocol retained 1,500 training tiles per species except C. elegans, for which the independent coordinate pool expanded to 3,000. Species-balanced training used 4,000 updates; the final checkpoint was evaluated. Each 8,192-bp tile was encoded as two independent 4,096-bp halves with native six-base tokens and explicit tail handling. A shared six-species CAL fit supplied Platt parameters and one deployment threshold, 0.42330056285498807. This manuscript does not retune it by species or result. The original chromosome allocation and computational positive/unknown/negative priority are documented in the [upstream protocol](../../experiments/CROSS-SPECIES-L1-UPSTREAM-20260904.md). Internal DEV comprises already observed coordinates from training species; it is not a family- or homology-isolated external test. Previously completed seed replications remain historical evidence, while the current supplements use seed 42 only.

Strict TE material comprised the comparator classes LINE, SINE, LTR, DNA, RC and Retroposon. Computationally unresolved sequence was excluded according to the experiment-specific callable mask. Source annotations, masked input and model predictions are separate assets. All counts and the scope of absent classes are retained with the corresponding results. Shared D external evidence uses only the panels described in Results; other reserved panels were not opened for this study extension. P3 denotes a separately trained GENERanno-derived model and is never treated as interchangeable with D.

### Evaluation and annotation sensitivity

Base-level precision, recall and F1 were computed from pooled callable TP/FP/FN counts within each reported species or panel. Species-macro values average species endpoints. L2 matching uses contiguous comparator runs with an IoU threshold of 0.8; the legacy joint-boundary endpoint additionally requires both matched boundaries within the stated tolerance. These reference runs are not biological insertion truth. Positive-only references permit reference-positive recovery, but do not qualify precision or F1 on all remaining genomic sequence.

The retrospective prevalence diagnostic holds each species' reference recall r and reference-negative FPR f fixed and computes F1(pi)=2*pi*r/[pi*(1+r)+(1-pi)*f] over pi=0.01 to 0.99. We also display the three-species pooled prevalence, without selecting it to maximize any endpoint. Original scores and labels are unchanged. Under the restrictive FP-only omitted-label hypothesis, the maximum F1 is 2(TP+FP)/[2(TP+FP)+FN]. This bound assumes existing positive labels and predictions remain fixed and no additional FN are exposed. Proportional label thinning is an analytical expected-count illustration, not a measurement of missing-label rates or an executed randomized deletion experiment.

The human library experiment uses the fixed inputs, two library configurations and no-reuse matching recorded in its [protocol](../../experiments/ANNOTATION-LIBRARY-CONTROL-20260915.md). Library agreement and RepeatPeps evidence are reported separately. The new three-species same-release comparison is prospectively fixed with respect to its new outputs but retrospective with respect to original DEV scores; it remains pending. Its [protocol](../../experiments/SPECIES-LIBRARY-CONTROL-20260916.md) specifies centre/halo coordinates, frozen prediction replay, complete confusion transitions, library-increment controls and finite stopping conditions.

### Representation and class/status experiments

The six-arm representation experiment contrasts raw six-mer features, raw frozen NTv2 embeddings, two standard SSL heads, a head-capacity control and updates to the final two native NTv2 blocks. Training uses two views of the same sequence and no family labels in its contrastive objective. Upstream selection of annotated natural copies means the experiment remains TE-enriched. Splitting excludes previously viewed records and associated recorded homology/locus neighbours. The common cluster count is selected without EVAL family labels. Transductive and inductive clustering are evaluated separately; full details and all arms are in the [representation protocol](../../experiments/NTV2-LABELFREE-CLUSTER-20260915.md).

SF5 uses six species with equal 900/240/360 train/validation/test window allocations and eight explicit class/status labels. The full validation set selects the checkpoint. The complete test set is evaluated once under the fixed ontology, with all class supports disclosed. The retained six-label baseline is collapsed for descriptive comparison rather than retrained as a matched causal control. Coordinates, label semantics and settings are in the [classification protocol](../../experiments/SF5-ONTOLOGY-CLOSURE-20260915.md).

### Long-input benchmark

We evaluated 100 Mb of TE_Bench/GARLIC-derived simulation and the complete CB4 assembly. Simulation truth comes from generation records after the strict TE-material definition and coordinate/sequence checks; biological insertion and strand endpoints were not qualified. Actual generating-library exposure is reported rather than hidden. Reference-assisted fixed RepeatMasker and EarlGrey are distinguished from discovery workflows and their bundled classification resources. Five native CPU methods and D on CPU/GPU form fourteen input-by-method cells. CPU measurements record 16 threads, the host class, actual elapsed time, memory and failed or resumed stages. Device-specific GPU costs are separate. Finite compatibility repairs retain historical attempts and accumulated costs. A failed biological candidate gate remains a failure with null accuracy rather than zero F1. The [benchmark protocol](../../experiments/TE-LONG-BENCH-20260915.md) and result ledgers define the complete denominator. A pinned Omnibenchmark graph recomputes metrics from qualified compact block counts; it does not measure native execution time or substitute for annotation qualification.

### Downstream gene prediction and uncertainty

P3 masks, reference-derived masks and unmasked inputs are compared with fixed Tiberius workflows and matched uppercase genomic sequence. A correctly recovered locus requires exact agreement with an eligible reference CDS chain under the frozen scorer. Counts include false predictions, eligible reference loci, and gained or lost formerly correct loci. The human analysis uses its original paired-core resampling and 1% loss safeguard. The external study contains 20 five-Mb cores per species, sampled on ten autosomes with 100-kb context flanks, and five arms per core. U_soft uses the same masking-aware checkpoint as P; U_nosm uses the official unmasked checkpoint, so that comparison is a workflow contrast. R_TE and R_all use the same native reference-annotation source with different mask definitions. All 200 cells and input/output invariants must qualify before scoring. External intervals use 10,000 within-species paired chromosome-cluster bootstrap draws with seed 42. These intervals describe sampled genomic units rather than training-seed uncertainty or annotation truth. Full eligibility, empty-subset conventions and gain/loss rules are in the [external protocol](../../experiments/P3-TIBERIUS-EXTERNAL-20260915.md).
